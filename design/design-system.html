The cilica.si system

The site is built from the app's own design system, scaled up for a page people scroll. Same tokens, springs, glass rules and voice. What's new here: a larger type scale, the ridgeline as the site's one piece of art, and live product mocks in place of screenshots.

Version 0.10.0

Notes that tidy themselves, with your say-so.

Cilica is a Markdown notes app for Mac, Windows and Linux. Its assistant can read and edit every note, and shows you each change before it sticks. Calls become notes too, transcribed on your computer, with no bot in the room.

What competitors do

I scrolled the full landing pages of Granola, Anarlog, Obsidian, Reflect, Notion's meeting notes and Craft, and checked each against Cilica's features. What each one leads with, how it looks, and what the Cilica site keeps or avoids:

Granola

Meeting notes, the market leader
Leads with
"The AI notepad for back-to-back meetings." No bot is the second line. Then before, during and after the meeting, chat over every meeting, then customer logos and quotes.
Looks
Typewriter-style serif display, warm off-white paper, olive and lime fields, app windows set on photographic landscapes, a sticky contents list that tracks scroll.
Keep
One small, calm app window per idea. The landscape behind the product, which our ridgeline does without a photo.
Avoid
The serif-on-paper look, which every AI notetaker now copies. Logo walls we can't fill yet.

Nobody shows the assistant editing

Every competitor's assistant answers questions or drafts emails. None shows it changing your notes, with a diff you approve and an Undo. Cilica can, so that leads.

"No bot" is now table stakes

Granola and Anarlog both lead with it. Cilica matches it in one line, then wins on what they can't show: speaker labels on your computer and voices it learns.

Nobody joins "your files" and "your model"

Obsidian has open files with no meetings. Anarlog and Reflect let you bring your own key, but aren't full notes apps. Cilica does all three.

Page structure

Three ways to build the page in this world. All three use the same components, sections and copy; they differ in what the scroll does. Pick one at the outline review, or tell me what to combine.

Structure A

Ridgeline descent

The page walks down the six ridges of the app icon. The hero stands in the sky at dawn (night in dark mode). Each section sits on the next ridge, a shade deeper. The page ends on the ink foreground, where the download lives.

Signature: as you scroll, the sun sinks behind the ridges, the ridges drift at their own depths, and the recorder light comes on as you reach meetings.

Risk: sand-coloured bands lean towards the warm-paper look many AI sites share. Graphite, blue and real UI have to keep it sharp, and ridge colour must never tint the product mocks.

Structure B

Element tiles

The Si tile becomes the page's grid. The hero is one large tile beside the app window. Features form a small periodic table grouped into families (notes, Cilica, meetings, voices, providers, details), and choosing a tile opens its live demo full width below.

Signature: tiles carry real figures in the corners the way elements carry atomic numbers: "3 s" behind speech, "94–99%" speaker labels, "<1 s" to open.

Risk: it can turn into a gimmick, and from a distance it reads as a bento grid, the category's default. Hiding demos behind tiles also means fewer people see them.

Structure C

One Cilica turn

The whole page is a single turn of the assistant. The hero is the composer with "Show me what you can do" typed in. Scrolling sends it, and each section arrives as a step ("Reading your notes…", "Joining your 10:00 call, no bot…", "Asking before I delete…") that opens into its demo. It ends on the "What changed" receipt, which holds the download.

Signature: the page uses the product's own grammar of steps, shimmer, and receipt from top to bottom.

Risk: a skimmer may take it for a chatbot page. The meeting story has to be told in the assistant's voice.

Also considered: the page as one long note; a pinned app window that the scroll drives through every feature; a day from dawn to night; the guard ladder as the spine. Any of these can come back.

Colour

Monochrome first, as in the app. Graphite does the layout. Each colour on the page has one meaning, taken from the app, so the site teaches the product's language: blue is Cilica working, red is recording, amber is your week, and the speaker colours are people. Values below follow the theme switch at the top.

Grounds and ink

Warm graphite, never pure black or white. Text uses ink, ink-2 for body copy, ink-3 for captions and timecodes.

Accent

One accent button per view, usually Download. Near-black in light, near-white in dark.

Signals

Cilica blue only appears where the assistant is working: the glow under the composer, the reading marker, the text it just touched. Amber belongs to Home's faces and the sun. Red is recording and nothing else.

Speakers

Only on avatar rings, name dots and voiceprints. Transcript text stays ink.

Ridgeline

The icon's six ridges, from sky to foreground. They're used only in the scene art and the section bands of Structure A, never behind body text below ridge 3.

Type

Inter, the face the app bundles, with optical sizing on so large sizes tighten the way SF Display does. Using the app's face keeps the site and the product reading as one thing. Geist Mono is only for timecodes, figures and keys. Newsreader, the app's optional note serif, appears only inside note mocks that show the serif setting. Open decisions

Things to settle at this review, before I write the section outline and copy.

  • Page structure. A, B or C, or a mix.
  • Hero headline. One of the three, or a direction for another.
  • Import count. The README says 45 apps; I counted about 39 provider ids, including "files". I'll check it against the code before the page names a number.
  • Swear jar. It's in "The details" as one tile with safe-for-work wording ("Coins drop when someone swears"). Keep it, or leave it for the app to reveal?
  • Hosting and analytics. Static Astro builds anywhere. I'd suggest Cloudflare Pages with Plausible or Umami (cookie-free, so no banner). Your call on both.
  • Download links. Where installers live (GitHub Releases?) so the button can point at the latest one per system.
